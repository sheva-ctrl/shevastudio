How to turn pushback on AI into people actually using it

Step 1 Watch the lesson · 6 min

Where Do I Start With AI? Build a Practical Foundation — includes the two rules that keep you safe

Step 2 Read it and try it ↓

The team that pushed back hardest on AI ended up using it the most. They weren't saying no. They were asking fair questions:

  • Where does our information go?
  • Who can see it?
  • What gets saved?

Here's how

  1. Ask what they're actually worried about.The most careful people ask the sharpest questions.
  2. Answer the information questions first.Where it goes, who sees it, what is kept.
  3. Write the answers on one page, in plain words.People follow rules they can find.

Once the rules were written down, the pushback stopped.

Try it this week: ask your team their #1 worry about AI, then write down the answer.

How to ask AI for something and get an answer you can use

Step 1 Watch the lesson · 5 min

How to Talk to AI So It Actually Understands You

Step 2 Read it and try it ↓

Vague answers come from vague requests. Most of us have never had to write down exactly what we want.

Here's how — say these five things, in order

  1. GoalWhat you want done.
  2. ContextThe background it needs.
  3. AudienceWho will read it.
  4. FormatA list, an email, a table.
  5. CheckAsk what it assumed before you trust the answer.

Most people skip audience and format. Add those two and the answer gets usable.

Try it this week: rewrite one request using all five lines and compare the answers.

How to use AI's first draft without getting burned

Step 1 Watch the lesson · 2 min

How to Use AI at Work Without Losing Your Judgment

Step 2 Read it and try it ↓

AI writes a first draft 10 times faster than I can. That's the easy part. The real work is checking it — and that's where the saved time should go.

Here's how

  1. Never send the first version.It's a starting point, not the finish.
  2. Check every number and every source.That's where AI is wrong while sounding sure.
  3. Keep the judgment calls.Tone, risk, and who hears what stay with you.

AI drafts it. You check it. You decide.

Try it this week: before you send anything AI wrote, check every number in it.

How to pick an AI helper that keeps working

Step 1 Watch the lesson · 5 min

How to Start Using AI: A No-Jargon Beginner's Guide — includes building one small workflow

Step 2 Read it and try it ↓

An "agent" is AI that does one job on its own, without you asking each time. Across 50+ AI projects, the big, exciting ones stalled. The boring ones are still running: a weekly summary, a folder that sorts itself, a monthly report.

Here's how

  1. Start with something boring.A weekly summary. A messy folder.
  2. Give it one thing that tells it to start.When you click, at a set time, or when something new arrives. Pick one.
  3. Test it on a copy first.So a mistake costs you nothing.

Small and boring beats big and exciting.

Try it this week: name the most boring task you repeat every week. That's your first one.
