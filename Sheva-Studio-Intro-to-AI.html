How to Talk to AI So It Actually Understands You

A good request has these pieces. Tap any one to open an example.

Role (optional). What role or personality do you want it to answer in? e.g. "Answer as a patient teacher," or "a friendly nurse."
Who you are and what's going on. e.g. "I'm a parent with no time to cook."
What you actually want it to do. e.g. "Plan three easy weeknight dinners."
How you want the answer to look. e.g. "A short list I can screenshot."
ExampleI run a small bakery. Write a warm, under-100-word email telling customers we're closed Monday for a staff day. No corporate tone, no bullet points.
Steal these
  • "Answer as a patient teacher: explain [topic] to me with one analogy and one real example."
  • "Here's a draft: [paste]. Tell me what's weak and how to fix it."
  • "Give me 10 varied ideas for [thing]. List only, no explanations yet."

Where Do I Start With AI? Build a Practical Foundation

  1. Open a tool and sign in.
    Not sure which? The tool picker is at the top of this guide. Free tiers are plenty to start.
  2. Tell it who you are and what you want.
    One or two sentences of context beats a clever keyword. "I'm a [role]. Help me [do this]. Keep it [short/casual/etc.]."
  3. React — don't restart.
    Steer the answer instead of starting over: "shorter," "more casual," "redo that part," "give me three versions."

Feeling Overwhelmed by AI? Start Here

  • Set your preferences once. In settings, tell it your role, your tone, and 2–3 things to avoid. It loads into every chat so you stop repeating yourself.
  • Turn on memory. It carries context between chats so you stop repeating yourself — and you can view or edit anything it keeps.
  • Build one small workflow. Pick a task you do every week and let it own the boring part. One habit beats ten experiments.

The one rule

Verify before you trust. AI sounds certain even when it's wrong. For anything that matters — numbers, names, legal or medical specifics — check it yourself. Use it to draft and think, not as the final word.
