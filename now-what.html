The tool isn't the question anymore

You already know which tool fits. And often it's picked for you anyway — your work chose one for security, and at home you use whatever's built into your phone.

Here's the freeing part

Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok — for everyday stuff they all do the same core things. Read, write, summarize, brainstorm, organize. So the real question isn't "which one?" It's "what do I actually hand to it?"

The whole skill

Spotting the handoff. It works no matter which tool you have, or how new you are to this.

What makes a good handoff

Good handoffs fit one of four patterns. Once you can spot them, you see them everywhere.

Repetitive

You do it over and over — weekly reports, similar emails, meal planning.

Time-consuming, low-stakes

Eats your time, but a rough first draft is enough to get going.

Brain-draining

The blank page. The thing you keep procrastinating on.

Translation work

Turning one form into another — messy notes into a clean summary.

The magic question

"Am I the only one who can do this — or do I just happen to be the one doing it?" If AI could take the first pass and you edit, that's a handoff.

What AI shouldn't do

Watch · 1:57

How to Use AI at Work Without Losing Your Judgment

Handing off a task doesn't mean handing off responsibility. You stay the editor, the decider, the human.

Final decisions

AI drafts. You decide. Always.

Sensitive info

At work, follow your organization's rules. At home, keep passwords and account numbers out of it.

Anything unverified

AI can sound confident and be wrong. If you can't check it, don't ship it.

The human touch

Hard conversations, condolences, your kid's birthday card. AI helps you start; you make it real.

Rule of thumb

AI is a very capable intern — not a replacement for your judgment.

One handoff, start to finish

The point isn't the polish — it's the time you get back.

The messy input — sound familiar?
"Team meeting today — talked about the new training program everyone's been asking for, budget approval needs to happen first but timeline is tight, someone suggested phases, legal probably needs to review, want to launch before the holidays but January's the backup."
The handoff
"Turn these notes into a short summary for my manager, plus a list of action items with owners."
What comes back, in seconds
Summary: The team is ready to move forward on the new training program, which has strong internal demand. Before launch, we need budget approval and a legal review. A phased rollout was proposed to manage the tight timeline. Target launch is before the holidays, with January as backup.

Action items: secure budget approval · send materials to legal · draft phased rollout plan · confirm launch window.
The real win

That's 15 minutes of your Friday afternoon, handed off. Now multiply it across everything you tapped above.

You came curious. Leave capable.

Once handoffs feel natural, the same skill scales up — setting AI loose on whole multi-step tasks while you supervise. That's agents. Start with one handoff; you're already on the path.

One prompt trick
