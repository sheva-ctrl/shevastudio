01 · The Moment We're All In

The tool isn't the question anymore.

You know which tool fits. This year, the tool is often already chosen for you anyway.

At work: your organization picked one based on security and guardrails — you might not even get a vote.
At home: you're using whatever's built into your phone, browser, and apps.

Here's the good news: Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok — for the everyday stuff, they all do the same core things. Read, write, summarize, brainstorm, organize.

So the real question isn't "which one?"
It's "what do I actually hand to it?" That's the whole skill. And it works no matter which tool you have, or how new you are to this.
02 · The One Skill

What makes a good handoff?

Look for tasks that fit one of these four patterns. Once you can spot them, you'll see handoffs everywhere.

Repetitive

You do it over and over — weekly reports, similar emails, meal planning.

Time-consuming, low-stakes

Eats your time, but a rough first draft is enough to get going.

Brain-draining

The blank page. The thing you keep procrastinating on.

Translation work

Turning one form into another — messy notes into a clean summary.

The magic question:
"Am I the only one who can do this — or do I just happen to be the one doing it?" If AI could take the first pass and you edit, that's a handoff.
03 · Keep These In Your Hands

What AI shouldn't do.

Handing off a task doesn't mean handing off responsibility. You stay the editor, the decider, the human.

Final decisions

AI drafts. You decide. Always.

Sensitive info

At work, follow your organization's rules. At home, keep passwords and account numbers out of it.

Anything unverified

AI can sound confident and be wrong. If you can't check it, don't ship it.

The human touch

Hard conversations, condolences, your kid's birthday card. AI helps you start; you make it real.

Rule of thumb:
AI is a very capable intern — not a replacement for your judgment.

06 · Watch It Work

One handoff, start to finish.

The messy input — sound familiar?

"Team meeting today — talked about the new training program everyone's been asking for, budget approval needs to happen first but timeline is tight, someone suggested doing it in phases, legal probably needs to review, everyone's excited, want to launch before the holidays but January's the backup."

The handoff:

"Turn these notes into a short summary for my manager, plus a list of action items with owners."

What comes back, in seconds:

Sample output

Summary for manager: The team is ready to move forward on the new training program, which has strong internal demand. Before launch, we need budget approval and a legal review of materials. A phased rollout was proposed to manage the tight timeline. Target launch is before the holidays, with January as a backup.

Action items:

  • Secure budget approval — owner: TBD
  • Send materials to legal for review — owner: TBD
  • Draft phased rollout plan & timeline — owner: TBD
  • Confirm launch window (holidays vs. January) — owner: TBD
The point isn't the output — it's the time.
That's 15 minutes of your Friday afternoon, handed off. Now multiply that across everything on the last two sections.

09 · Keep Going

You came curious. Leave capable.

And the next step? Agents.
Once handoffs feel natural, the same skill scales up — setting AI loose to handle whole multi-step tasks while you supervise. That's where this is heading. Start with one handoff; you're already on the path.
  • Just start asking. The best way to learn is to hand off something small — today.
  • Whatever tool you have, search "[your tool] + getting started" for the official basics.
  • One prompt trick: end any request with "ask me questions first if you need more info." It makes AI dramatically more useful.
